Technical details and model tests / Detalle técnico y pruebas del modelo

Team TROYA · UNAM, Moquegua, Peru · NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 · Challenge: Be An Earth System Trend Detective!

Why this challenge: we detect trends in four parts of the Earth system for Moquegua (atmosphere: 2 m temperature; hydrosphere: rain and frost; ocean: El Niño/ONI and Niño 1+2; land: the farms that depend on them) with 45 years of NASA data, test whether they are real (Mann-Kendall, Sen slope) and turn them into monthly decisions for low-literacy farmers.
Por qué este reto: detectamos tendencias en el sistema Tierra de Moquegua (aire, agua, océano y suelo agrícola) con 45 años de datos de la NASA, comprobamos si son reales y las convertimos en decisiones para el agricultor.

1. Data / Datos

  • NASA POWER (MERRA-2 based): monthly precipitation and 2 m temperature, 1981-2025, for 6 points in Moquegua; daily data for the last 30 days.
  • NASA GPM IMERG (via Giovanni): monthly satellite precipitation to validate POWER, and daily Late Run to verify the last 30 days (POWER's latest days are provisional and overestimated rain 3-8× in the lowlands).
  • NOAA CPC: ONI (central Pacific) and Niño 1+2 (off Peru) indices.
  • Cleaning: 18 false rain months removed (more than 3× the month's 95th percentile and above 30 mm), validated against GPM.

1b. What updates by itself every day / Lo que se actualiza solo cada día

  • In each farmer's phone, once a day: the app downloads the NASA POWER daily series 1981-today for the area (T2M, T2M_MAX, T2M_MIN, PRECTOTCORR) and recomputes "Today in your area": temperature and frost nights of the last 30 days against the same dates of every year, and the fact of the day. Only the results are stored on the phone.
  • The next-month temperature model is retrained every day in the phone with every complete month up to today (same regression as the notebook: month + trend + ONI; about 550 months). Checked against the notebook: Coast, December 2026 → 21.2 °C (notebook 21.1 °C).
  • A GitHub Actions robot (.github/workflows/actualizar_datos.yml) downloads the NOAA ONI every morning into oni.json, because NOAA does not allow direct download from a web page.
  • Rain from the GPM IMERG satellite, every day: the same robot downloads NASA GPM IMERG Late daily rain (GPM_3IMERGDL.07, GES DISC OPeNDAP, with the team's Earthdata token stored as a GitHub secret) for the 6 areas, keeps the daily series since 2001 in imerg_diario.json and writes imerg.json: rain of the last 30 days and its percentiles on the same dates of every year. The app uses it for "Rain this month" when it is less than 10 days old.
  • Backup: if IMERG is not available, the app uses POWER rain; POWER's last days are provisional and sometimes far too high, so a doubtful value (more than 3× the 95th percentile and above 30 mm) is replaced by the latest IMERG check if it is less than 45 days old; otherwise the app says it is not sure.
  • The summer 2027 forecast and the 2027-2037 projection are seasonal products: they are recomputed with the notebook once per season, not daily.

Cada día, en el celular: se bajan los datos diarios de NASA POWER, se recalcula "Hoy en su zona" y se re-entrena el modelo de temperatura del próximo mes. Un robot de GitHub actualiza cada mañana el índice de El Niño (ONI) y la lluvia del satélite GPM IMERG.

3. Does the model work? / ¿Funciona el modelo?

Temperature = month effect + linear trend + ONI. Rain (monthly) = month effect + month-specific ONI effect. Linear regression (least squares), compared against the historical monthly average and a Random Forest.

Rolling-origin test (train up to year X, predict X+1, X+5, X+10). Temperature beats the historical average at all horizons; monthly rain does not, so the app only shows normal ranges for rain.

Accuracy and overfitting (train 1981-2012, test 2013-2025)

Overfitting flag: test RMSE > 1.5 × train RMSE. Random Forest overfits; it is not used.

Sensitivity tests

Summer (Dec-Mar) forecast: which El Niño index works? (improvement % over the average, 28 test summers 1998-2025)

Summer forecast is shown only where it beats the average and the "always normal" baseline (Coast: +12%, right 46% vs 32%). Desert: weak signal. Other areas: not forecastable. For summer 2027 the El Niño index is beyond the 1981-2025 range, so the app shows the 5 most similar summers instead of a mm figure.

4. Limitations / Límites

  • Monthly rain is not forecast month by month nor years ahead.
  • Recent temperature (last 30 days) is provisional NASA POWER data, updated daily.
  • The temperature projection to 2037 assumes neutral ENSO; next-month temperature assumes the current El Niño persists.
  • Each NASA POWER point represents ~50 km; it does not replace a local station. Future work: SENAMHI stations and PISCO.

5. Sources of the farmer advice / Fuentes de los consejos

  • Frost: FAO (Snyder & de Melo-Abreu), Frost Protection: fundamentals, practice and economics; University of Georgia Extension (moist soil stores heat; covers before sundown).
  • Animal shelters: MIDAGRI / Agro Rural, cobertizos reduce alpaca and sheep mortality; priority to young and pregnant animals.
  • Skin: MINSA / SENAMHI UV advice (hats, long sleeves, sunscreen); EsSalud (cold dries the skin; moisturize).
  • Water saving: mulch, drip and cool-hour irrigation reduce evaporation; Penn State Extension (with drip, time of day matters less; soil moisture matters most).
  • Canals: National Water Authority (ANA), lined canals reduce seepage losses; conveyance efficiency 71% unlined vs 84% lined (Peru case study).
  • Qochas and infiltration trenches: MIDAGRI – Fondo Sierra Azul, 77 qochas and 21.5 km of trenches in Moquegua.
  • Crops of Moquegua: BCRP regional economic reports (alfalfa, avocado, grape, oregano, potato, olive).
  • Olive: winter cold needed for flower induction; 2024 El Niño heat and the olive harvest in Tacna.
  • Avocado: high temperatures reduce fruit set. Grape: UC Davis, berry sunburn under heat waves.
  • Potato: most varieties are damaged below about −2.5 °C (IGP); water stress affects tuber formation (INIA–CIP, FONTAGRO).
  • Heat and sun: MINSA, avoid direct sun 10:00–16:00, light clothing, hydration; outdoor workers: hat, SPF 30+ every 2 hours.
  • Cold season health: MINSA, influenza and pneumococcal vaccination during low temperatures.
  • Heavy rain: INDECI, stay away from rivers and ravines; drainage of low plots.
  • Livestock in frost: gob.pe, shelters for pregnant females and young; hay stored dry and roofed.
  • Alfalfa: summer slump in heat; extra irrigation does not compensate. Olive: flowering is the most sensitive stage to water deficit.
  • "Fact of the day" (live): the app queries the NASA POWER daily API (T2M_MAX, T2M_MIN, PRECTOTCORR) when opened, takes the latest available day (about 2–3 days behind) and compares it with the same calendar date in 1981–2025 and with the 1991–2020 normal (±3-day window). Recent days come from NASA's near-real-time product, so small differences with the historical record are possible. Without internet it falls back to precomputed facts.
  • Languages: the app suggests Quechua (Collao variety) or Aymara according to the zone, following the Ministry of Culture's list of predominant Indigenous languages (RM 000122-2025-MC: Mariscal Nieto, Aymara and Quechua; General Sánchez Cerro, Quechua; Ilo, Aymara). The user can always change it. The Quechua and Aymara texts of the main screen are a draft under review by native speakers from Moquegua; voice and long texts remain in Spanish until reviewed.
  • Andean knowledge card ("Did you know?"): Orlove, Chiang & Cane (2000), Nature 403:68–71, Pleiades visibility and El Niño, analysed with NASA ISCCP and SAGE II data (NASA Earth Observatory); waru waru (AFP 2024; World Monuments Fund Peru); chuño (Cultural Survival); terraces (Agraria.pe); elders' signs, shown as tradition and not as verified forecasts (fox, Puno; karigua, RPP).
  • Oregano: needs less water than maize or alfalfa; yield fell 50% with lack of rain. Pastures: frost and drought limit forage growth (CIES).

The advice is general guidance and should be validated by a local agronomist.
